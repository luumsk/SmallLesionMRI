Small MS lesions can be missed by automated segmentation models whose
training rewards voxel overlap, even when their Dice score is high. Adding
lesion-level terms to the standard nnU-Net training objective, without
changing the network, reduced the number of completely missed lesions by
about half on MSLesSeg and improved the detection of lesions of at least
3~mm, the size considered in the 2024 McDonald criteria and MAGNIMS--CMSC--NAIMS
recommendations, on two independent datasets (94.4\% against 87.0\% and
89.4\% against 83.0\%). The gain was reproduced on 47 further patients and
came from the term that penalizes completely missed lesions. Its cost,
additional small false-positive detections, depended on the dataset: a
simple size filter removed most of them, but the filter had to be larger
on 3D-MR-MS and must be adjusted to the data and the intended use. Because
a reader can dismiss a false positive but cannot review a lesion that is
never shown, this trade-off makes lesion-level training a candidate for a
second reader that flags lesions for radiologist confirmation. A reader
study and a longitudinal evaluation of new lesions are needed to establish
its clinical benefit.
