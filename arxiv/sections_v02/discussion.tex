\paragraph{Why standard objectives miss small lesions.}
The results point to a specific failure. The baselines do not miss small
lesions narrowly; for most of them the network produces no response at all,
so the omission is fixed during training and cannot be undone at
inference. Four other architectures trained with the same loss show the
same omission, while a change of objective with the network held fixed
removes much of it. Within the range of networks tested, the omission is
therefore better explained by the supervision than by the capacity of the
network. \Cref{eq:sl_voxel_loss} gives the reason: a lesion of a few voxels
contributes a few terms to a sum over hundreds of thousands, and an
optimizer has little incentive to fit it.

\paragraph{Why both balance and detection.}
The two terms of CATMIL answer two distinct deficits of a voxel-wise loss.
Balance alone, whether through the CAT term, CC-DiceCE, or a heavier
penalty on false-negative voxels, raises small-lesion recall only part of
the way (\cref{sec:ablation}). The form of the terms suggests why. In an
overlap term, even one balanced across lesions, a lesion enters through its
soft recall $r_k$ linearly (\cref{eq:sl_cat_lesion_form}): a completely
missed lesion and a half-segmented lesion of the same weight receive the
same marginal gradient, so nothing in the objective marks a missed lesion
as a different kind of error. The MIL term does. Its contribution,
$-\log s_k$, grows steeply as the strongest response in a lesion approaches
zero, its gradient $1/(K s_k)$ is largest for exactly those lesions, and it
vanishes once a single voxel is confident. Conversely, the MIL term says
nothing about how much of a lesion is segmented; that is left to the
overlap terms, and the CAT term ensures that this delineation signal is
not dominated by large lesions. Detection and delineation are thereby
supervised separately. This division is visible in the results: the
recovered lesions are detected but often only partly delineated, and the
gain persists, though narrowing, under stricter coverage criteria
(\cref{appendix:strict-detection}). In the present ablation the detection
gain is carried by the MIL term, and a measurable benefit of the CAT term
on delineation was not shown.

This interpretation has a limit. Against the Tversky-based losses, much of
the gain disappears at matched precision (\cref{sec:matched_precision}), so
those losses can reach a similar sensitivity by a lower threshold or filter
size. What the lesion-level terms change reliably is where the default operating point lies, and,
against DiceCE and CC-DiceCE, the detection achievable at a given
precision.

\paragraph{Relation to instance-level losses.}
Instance-level losses~\cite{shirokikh_universal_2020,kofler_blob_2023,bouteille_learning_2026}
were developed for the same problem and do improve small-lesion recall;
CC-DiceCE did so here, at some cost in precision, as its authors
reported~\cite{bouteille_learning_2026}. CATMIL is complementary rather
than an alternative to them: its CAT term follows the same balancing
principle, and the MIL term adds what balancing lacks, a lesion-level
existence signal that is independent of overlap. Combining this signal
with instance-level losses other than the CAT term, or with
BiCC~\cite{bouteille_bicc_2026}, which penalizes unmatched predicted
components, is a direct next step.

\paragraph{The trade-off and its control.}
The detection gain and the false-positive cost arise from the same term:
a loss that rewards one confident voxel inside every lesion also makes the
network respond to small lesion-like foci elsewhere. The resulting
components are small and mostly close to true lesions, so they add little
false-positive volume and can be removed by a size filter after training.
This makes the operating point adjustable without retraining. The filter
size, however, depends on the data: a five-voxel filter sufficed on
MSLesSeg but not on 3D-MR-MS, and a small residual precision cost remains
on the MSLesSeg cross-validation patients. Moving this control into the
objective, for instance by penalizing predicted components that overlap no
reference lesion, would act on exactly the error that the MIL term
introduces.

\paragraph{Clinical implications.}
In MS, a lesion that is never detected cannot be counted, whereas a
false-positive component can be dismissed when a reader reviews the
segmentation. Where automatic segmentation supports rather than replaces
the radiologist, trading missed lesions for small false positives is
therefore a favorable direction, with the filter size chosen for the use:
small for screening and follow-up, larger where a clean lesion map is
needed. The gain is not confined to lesions below the size used in
clinical reading. It is largest for small lesions of at least 3~mm, which
are the lesions that are counted and in which the central vein sign, a
diagnostic marker in the 2024 McDonald criteria, can be
assessed~\cite{montalban_diagnosis_2025,barkhof_2024_2025}.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
First, the evaluation is restricted to MS, and the objective has not been
tested on other pathologies. Second, CATMIL was trained only with nnU-Net;
the other architectures were trained with DiceCE, with default
hyperparameters, a 150-epoch budget, and only on MSLesSeg, so it is not
known whether CATMIL raises their recall as much. Among instance-level
losses, only CC-DiceCE was trained under the same protocol, using the
authors' loss within this pipeline rather than their own setup. Third, the
test sets are small (six patients each), so no paired test can reach
$p < 0.05$ after correction, and the analysis rests on patient-level CIs
and on consistency across patients; on 3D-MR-MS, one patient also
contributes 64\% of the reference lesions. The 47 cross-validation
patients of MSLesSeg reproduce the main findings, but the corresponding
analysis was not performed on 3D-MR-MS. Fourth, the operating points at
matched precision were selected on the MSLesSeg test set, so only their
transfer to 3D-MR-MS is free of selection. Fifth, the ablation was run on
MSLesSeg only, where the contribution of the CAT term was not measurable;
its role should be tested on further datasets.
Sixth, connected components are computed within each training patch, so a
lesion cut by the patch border is weighted by its visible part. Finally,
lesions of a few voxels and lesions with low contrast remain difficult for
every loss: the objective changes how much each lesion counts during
training, but it cannot create evidence that the image does not contain.
